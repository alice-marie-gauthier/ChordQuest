Song progression
  • Random practice — chords are drawn randomly, set up by:
    • Chord categories — pick which to include below (open any one of them to narrow it down further to specific qualities):
      • Major — bright, simple triads: root, major 3rd, and 5th (e.g. C major = C-E-G).
      • Minor — darker, moodier triads: root, minor 3rd, and 5th (e.g. C minor = C-Eb-G).
      • 7th Chords — four-note chords: a triad plus a 7th stacked on top (e.g. C dominant 7 = C-E-G-Bb).
      • Suspensions — a 2nd or 4th replaces the 3rd, so there's no major/minor color (e.g. Csus4 = C-F-G).
      • Extensions — notes added on top of the basic triad or 7th chord: 6th, 9th, 11th, 13th, and add4/add9.
      • Inversions — the same chord, but a note other than the root sits lowest (e.g. C/E is C major with E in the bass).
    • Recognition mode — Held chord: play every note at once, like a real chord. Arpeggio: play the notes one after another; the game remembers them briefly so it can still recognize the chord.
  • Custom progression — practice a real song's chords in order, from:
    • Upload CSV — download the blank template below, fill it in with your own chords, and upload it.
    • Library — or skip that and just pick an already-saved song from My Library / Uploaded Songs.

Input device
  • MIDI — connect a real MIDI keyboard (USB or Bluetooth) and the browser detects it automatically. Whatever notes you actually press are what gets recognized as the chord.
  • Computer keyboard — no MIDI keyboard needed: play with A W S E D F T G Z H U J K on your own keyboard, or tap the on-screen piano keys shown once this mode is active.
  • Speed — the slider just below sets how fast the chord track scrolls, shown as a BPM tempo; drag it any time, even mid-run.
